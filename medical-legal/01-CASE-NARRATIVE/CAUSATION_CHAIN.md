# Case Narrative & Causation

## Executive Summary

**Patient:** Brandy Michelle Bianchini

**Claim:** Systemic medical negligence across eight healthcare institutions resulting in:
- Failure to diagnose and coordinate care for genetic conditions
- Preventable emergency procedures and hospitalizations
- Head trauma and cervical spine injury without proper risk stratification
- Abandonment after injury despite known genetic predisposition
- Continued harm and coverage denial caused by provider-created record gaps

**Evidence Base:** 
- Maximus case review (expert findings)
- UCLA genomic profile breakthrough (proof of concept)
- 300+ sources of medical research and analysis
- Recorded communications showing institutional response failures
- Timeline of events with documented omissions

---

## The Causation Chain

### Stage 1: Baseline (2016-2017)
**What should have happened:** Primary care screening for family history of genetic disease

**What actually happened:** Family history of inherited disease not escalated or acted upon

**Consequence:** Genetic conditions remained undiagnosed and unmanaged

---

### Stage 2: Injury Event (Head Trauma + Cervical Spine)
**What should have happened:** Risk stratification for patient with scoliosis and degenerative disc disease; immediate escalation to appropriate specialists; coordination of care

**What actually happened:** 
- No connection made between injury severity and pre-existing fragility
- No coordination across providers
- No escalation of genetic risk factors

**Consequence:** Emergency procedures became necessary that might have been prevented with proper planning

---

### Stage 3: Emergency Procedures & Complications
**What should have happened:** Tailored care plan based on genomic profile; coordinated multidisciplinary team; documented protocols

**What actually happened:** 
- Ad-hoc emergency interventions
- No genomic profiling
- No care coordination
- Each institution operated in isolation

**Consequence:** Repeated hospitalizations, expensive emergency procedures, compounding harm

---

### Stage 4: Post-Injury Abandonment
**What should have happened:** Continued care coordination, referral to specialists, genomic research integration

**What actually happened:** 
- Providers "ran" from the case
- Referrals sent nowhere
- No follow-up on escalations
- Risk management contacted but took no action

**Consequence:** Patient forced to self-advocate; care gaps expanded; genetic research conducted independently

---

### Stage 5: UCLA Breakthrough
**What happened:** Independent genomic profiling at UCLA proved that tailored, genetics-informed care IS possible and effective

**Significance:** This is proof that provider failures were NOT inevitable — they were choices made by institutions that lacked coordination, expertise, or will

---

### Stage 6: Current Harm
**What should have happened:** Retroactive coordination of care; acknowledgment of negligence; remediation

**What actually happened:** 
- Coverage denial due to record gaps created by provider negligence
- Continued pain and disability
- No agency stepping in to audit or enforce accountability
- Patient left to compile her own case while in pain

**Consequence:** Escalating financial damages, continued medical harm, institutional impunity

---

## Why This Matters Legally

### Duty to Act
Each institution had a **duty** to:
1. Screen for inherited disease risk
2. Escalate findings to appropriate specialists
3. Coordinate care across providers
4. Document and follow up on recommendations
5. Respond to patient escalations

### Breach of Duty
Evidence shows:
- Primary care failed to screen family history
- Risk management contacted but did not act
- Referrals sent to wrong departments or not followed up
- No coordination across providers
- Communications ignored or dismissed

### Causation
The UCLA breakthrough proves:
- Tailored care is possible
- Provider failures were not inevitable
- Preventable harm occurred due to institutional choices

### Damages
- Preventable emergency procedures
- Unnecessary hospitalizations
- Out-of-pocket costs for travel, lodging, food
- Lost productivity and work
- Coverage denial caused by record gaps
- Continued pain and disability
